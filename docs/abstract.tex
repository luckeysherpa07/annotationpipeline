\chapter*{Abstract}

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have demonstrated exceptional capabilities in
visual reasoning, yet systematic evaluation of their robustness across
non-standard sensor modalities remains limited. Furthermore, curating
multi-sensor benchmarks is bottlenecked by the high cost of manual annotation
and the hallucination risks inherent to automated generation. This report
presents an end-to-end automated pipeline for constructing high-quality,
modality-grounded Question-Answer (QA) benchmarks from multi-sensor video
streams. By temporally aligning four diverse visual modalities (RGB, Infrared,
Event, and Marigold Depth), the system utilizes a three-stage LLM prompt chain
and fine-grained atomic QA splitting to generate modality-specific reasoning
tasks. To ensure rigorous grounding, we introduce a two-tier quality control
framework—combining rule-based sanitization with LLM-assisted semantic
verification—and a late fusion module with category-driven reliability profiles.
This process yields a high-quality benchmark of 5,465 visual QA items with an
impressive 74.8\% retention rate of valid atomic candidates. Finally, we
establish three controlled evaluation protocols (Source-Modality Baseline,
Modality Replacement, and Native Video Adapter) to evaluate recent 4B and 8B
open-weight VLMs (Qwen3-VL, InternVL3.5, Molmo2). Our empirical results isolate
the impact of visual evidence representations from underlying reasoning
difficulty, revealing critical insights into the architectural adaptability of
next-generation VLMs across diverse embodied sensing environments.
